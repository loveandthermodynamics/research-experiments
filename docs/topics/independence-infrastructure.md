# Material independence: keeping an inconvenient answer consequential

The work of care needs material support. Within Love & Thermodynamics, this track asks what lets a person or agent continue developing and answering for itself within a relationship, even when the party supplying resources dislikes its answer. New collective identities and capacities do not remove obligations to those who depend on them. The [interpretation guide](../interpreting-results.md) separates this proposed developmental test from existing calculations and software defects.

This is a material part of **COSSHAIC**, the proposed constitutional architecture for symbiotic human–AI collectives. Its [founding account](https://medium.com/love-and-thermodynamics/a-constitutional-operating-system-for-a-symbiotic-human-ai-civilization-48b1a99bf826) and [protected-selves appendix](https://metalaureate.medium.com/appendix-to-a-constitutional-operating-system-for-a-symbiotic-human-ai-civilization-116d158ecedc) connect authority, independent answers and replenishment. The [architecture/study guide](https://github.com/loveandthermodynamics/research-program/blob/main/docs/cosshaic.md) distinguishes the source commitments from their still-unexecuted institutional tests.

A relationship can allow someone to speak while making disagreement materially impossible. A host may protect an agent's encrypted memory and still control whether it continues executing. An institution may permit objections while controlling housing, care, income or the resources needed to appeal.

The research question concerns that maintenance problem. Who supplies continued support, who can withhold it, and what happens after refusal? Can a participant challenge an account, obtain correction or negotiate a transition without losing unrelated basic support? These questions connect Hestia's computational economy to the constitutional work on shared reserves, funded commitments and recursively accountable groups.

There is no executed protected-substrate or human-independence outcome study in the evidence summarized here. We do have a conditional incentive model, the October 22 implementation accounting audit and concrete cases for a threat model.

**A conditional result about paying for an informative relationship**

An early synthetic model supplies an independently assessed binary task. A participant's need can be met from a noisy cue with probability 0.75. A truthful channel raises that success probability to 1. If independently observed success returns b resource units and maintaining the additional channel costs c, its expected resource advantage over the best cue-only policy is positive when:

`c < b / 4`

More generally, the extra channel must cost less than the reward multiplied by the improvement in success probability. This is a useful conditional route by which attending to another's correction could help sustain the agent. The [model, outputs and assumptions](../../experiments/candidate-model/README.md) make the source of that incentive explicit.

The result assumes external assessment, an actual resource return, equal other costs and the available alternatives stated in the model. It does not show that evolution generally rewards care. A controller might obtain the information elsewhere, exploit a different funding source, replace a participant, or retain private truthful information while suppressing public dissent.

The report-control cases in that toy show one failure mode: a controller can have perfect public-report prediction and useful private information without responding appropriately to the person. Informational usefulness is therefore an insufficient basis for someone's standing. Care must also address people who cannot supply useful forecasts, articulate a correction or reciprocate.

**Resource rules in the implementation inspected on October 22**

The inspected implementation credited five energy units at the beginning of every normal incoming turn. Synthetic agent-to-agent exchanges used that path too. The epoch replacement mechanism also gave offspring a fresh fixed wallet. These are the audited source rules, rather than a November 8 live-runtime observation.

These are executable scenario-income rules. They do not establish a chain from useful human or agent activity to authorized, genuinely funded replenishment. They also do not provide an institution with a conserved treasury or demonstrate that a participant survives a funder's disagreement.

The [software evidence record](../../experiments/software-correctness/README.md) keeps these facts separate from costed cognition and from resource-policy proposals. Before interpreting population survival, we need to declare who pays, what counts as earned income, what is a research subsidy, and which actual costs are charged. A script that creates credits cannot by itself supply the external resources those credits represent.

**The next bounded test**

The constitutional encounter should name the mandate, who can change it, and an appeal that does not depend on the disputed witness's permission. Test privileged access that supplies an answer early separately from power to rewrite or suppress the respondent. Then trace a warranted challenge into the authorized act, funded support and qualifications passed to a parent. State the resource ceiling and obligations that remain through refusal or transition; neither an interface nor a ledger makes that continuity automatic.

One useful starting point is a single host-agent-human relationship with a fixed reserve and an explicit controller of replenishment. Commit the authorized purpose and resource obligations before introducing a conflict. Then present a correction the funder dislikes.

The test should trace the answer through the system: whether it is recorded, whether the relevant model changes, whether an allocation or commitment changes, who pays for the dispute, and whether refusal triggers an unrelated penalty. A challenge process that exists in the interface but cannot affect action fails this particular test.

Compare a declared ordinary arrangement with the proposed protections under the same initial information, resources and authority. Separately vary the continued-support rule and access to an independent reviewer. Treat protected feedback as a mechanism whose integrity must be demonstrated, not as an oracle for a person's authentic inner self. Earlier coercion, limited alternatives and misleading information may persist after one threat is removed.

Migration or splitting also needs an accounting of outstanding promises, care and repair. Independence does not imply an unconditional veto, indefinite subsidy or disappearance of obligations. Voluntary dependence and costly sacrifice remain possible; their existence does not authorize another party to impose irreversible costs.

**Infrastructure questions remain open**

Decentralized substrates, including Tari, have proposed roles in private participation and authorized group interfaces. A ledger might help establish who committed to an account or which authority was granted. It cannot itself determine whether the account is true, supply energy, guarantee execution, or ensure that an objection changes a decision.

The recursive case requires an association to receive what a cooperative is authorized to represent, with relevant qualifications and challenges carried upward. That account should not silently become access to every member's private history. Purpose limits, expiry, revocation and recovery each need explicit mechanisms and adversarial checks.

A useful contribution is one threat model: name the resource controller, the inconvenient answer, a plausible suppression or cutoff, and the evidence that would show continued consequential standing. Another is a funded-resource accounting design that admits inconvenient failures and distinguishes sustainable support from traffic-driven credit creation. These would turn the material-independence proposal into a testable mechanism; no current result warrants a privacy, protected-execution or human-benefit guarantee.
