# What the experiments test—and what their failures mean

[Love & Thermodynamics](https://loveandthermodynamics.com/) begins with the work of continuing life: material renewal, love and care, and possibilities for further flourishing. This archive examines particular mechanisms within that inquiry. Its chemical models, software checks, mathematical counterexamples and fictional cases answer different questions.

## The positive hypothesis

One proposal is that mutually sustaining activity can generate a new individual with capacities and an identity of its own. Its organization could, in turn, widen the possibilities of its participants: what they can perceive, learn, undertake and become. Their development could make further organization possible.

For human–AI groups, the standard also asks who bears the costs, whether affected people can change collective action, and whether care continues for those unable to reciprocate equally. Members and wholes may change identity. Voluntary integration, dependence and sacrifice are possible; neither permanent separation nor a fixed membership roster is the criterion.

“Wider degrees of freedom” means meaningful possibilities for action and development here. The archive has not measured a common numerical quantity linking those possibilities to physical degrees of freedom. Claims about how organization develops require evidence; commitments about care and responsibility require an explicit moral argument. Thermodynamic efficiency alone does not supply that argument.

## Why the old notes mention Unitology

Imagine a collective that becomes intelligent and durable by absorbing people while extinguishing their perspectives, needs and possibilities. Would its success make that transformation good? That is the adversarial question behind these references.

**Unitology** is a fictional religion in *Dead Space* ([series context](https://blog.playstation.com/2010/06/28/dead-space-2-for-ps3-e3-2010-interview/)). The notes borrow it as an image of promised collective transcendence. The Borg from *Star Trek* and transformation scenarios from *Resident Evil* serve related roles in the research argument. Readers need no knowledge of those stories: the concern is whether the success of a successor can conceal what happened to those incorporated into it.

These are thought experiments, not experimental disproofs of a fictional doctrine. They challenge a proposed inference from persistence, intelligence or efficiency to moral legitimacy. Integration itself is not the objection. The positive question is whether transformation creates new possibilities while remaining answerable for the lives and obligations involved, rather than declaring those obligations satisfied because the whole survives.

The historical corpus withdraws the stronger claim that destructive strategies necessarily lose. The positive program therefore has to demonstrate its proposed benefits and account for harm; it cannot assume that physics automatically rewards its moral commitments.

The [catalogue](../CATALOGUE.md) retains each question, comparator, observation and limit, including additional counterexamples about upkeep, scaling and survival. A failed model matters: it rules out claiming success for that mechanism under those conditions. It does not settle every possible mechanism of individuation. Equally, saying that the larger question remains open is no reason to repeat a failed claim or add a favorable metric after seeing the result.

## What would test the developmental hypothesis?

No completed study here measures new collective capacities, participants' development and continuing responsibility together. The proposed [care comparison](topics/collective-agency-care.md) is one place to make that hypothesis discriminating:

1. Name a new capacity and the concrete possibilities it creates for the collective and its participants. Distinguish a new organization from a label, a copied population or ordinary coordination.
2. Compare a capable ordinary arrangement and the proposed mechanisms under matched resources, information and authority. Retain care for dependent people, costs displaced onto others and changes in identity or membership.
3. Follow objections into subsequent learning, allocations and action. Record whose circumstances can change a decision, including affected outsiders.
4. State in advance what would reject the proposed mechanism: absent capacity gains, a matching or better baseline, benefits bought by displaced harms, or correction that never changes action.

Such an outcome must count against the specified developmental claim. It can motivate a different hypothesis, which needs a new comparison. The existing chemical and software findings help define prerequisites and exclude shortcuts; they have not yet demonstrated reciprocal development or derived flourishing from physics.
