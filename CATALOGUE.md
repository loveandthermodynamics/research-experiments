### P01 — Two-site finite-fuel renewal and finite-temperature availability

**Analytical recurrence with finite-matrix numerical checks; executed.**

Question: Can repairers with different vulnerabilities retain more active availability from the same finite fuel supply?

Method: Compare independent and common damage using an exact renewal recurrence in the irreversible limit, followed by reversible finite-temperature matrix calculations.

Declared run/check counts: sites: 2; initial fuel packets: 3; finite temperature cases: 3.

Finding:

- With epsilon=lambda=mu=1, fuel gap 2 and K=3 packets, integrated active energy is 2.875 under independent damage and 2 under common damage. Expected repairs are 0.875; heat 3.75 plus unused fuel energy 4.25 closes the initial energy inventory of 8.
- At theta=0.05 and horizon 4, active-availability integrals are approximately 2.5046 versus 1.8866. The evaluated positive bound is 0.4555 and the calculated advantage is 0.6180.
- In the separate one-packet coupling example, the conditional optimum g=0.06816723450754329 gives 2.056470757869438 integrated active-energy units, compared with 2 in isolation and 1.2222222222222223 at g=1.

Limits:

- Templates and the response map are supplied premises.
- First inactivity is an episode endpoint; rare thermal reactivation remains possible in the finite-temperature model.
- The availability bound is evaluated numerically rather than certified by interval arithmetic. The coupling optimum concerns trait performance, not demonstrated evolution.

### P03 — Equal-marginal correlated-shock comparison

**Finite-state numerical comparison with resolution refinement; executed.**

Question: Can different vulnerabilities leave a surviving repairer after a shock while preserving each site's marginal damage rate?

Method: Start with the same three active sites, zero contacts and 12 fuel packets. Compare 1, 2 and 3 vulnerability classes at the same per-site marginal shock rate.

Declared run/check counts: parameter scan cases: 27; refined reported arrangements: 3; refinement grids: [481, 1921].

Finding:

- At permeability 3 and horizon 30, integrated active availability is 67.9511, 92.5611 and 102.6467 for 1, 2 and 3 classes. Three classes gain 51.06% over one.
- Integrated active function is 19.6844, 27.9911 and 31.2866, a 58.94% gain for three classes. Fuel use is 0.0353, 2.2419 and 3.2389 packets.
- Initial total availability is matched at 96.3024. The successful arrangement consumes more fuel.

Limits:

- Integrated active availability is neither extracted work nor additional total energy.
- Aggregate all-of-class failures are supplied reversible channels rather than elementary chemical reactions.
- Finite-rate shocks do not establish fat-tail scaling.

### P05 — Two-patch chemical transport and regrowth

**Deterministic concentration simulations; executed.**

Question: Can a surviving chemical population physically recolonize a depleted patch?

Method: Use reversible assembly and production in two equal-volume patches. Apply targeted kinetic suppression, then a fixed interval of passive diffusion that transfers real material and depletes the donor.

Declared run/check counts: ODE cases: 17; patch volumes: [0.5, 0.5].

Finding:

- At horizon 120, integrated assembled C is 318.2655 for the diverse arrangement and 192.3093 for the equal-prior monoculture mean, a gain of 65.50%.
- Without transport, the ensemble means coincide to numerical precision.
- Retained controls show that low-feed indiscriminate mixing can destroy the diverse arrangement; common shocks and short shocks are also tested.

Limits:

- Patch geometry, the diffusion schedule and the response chemistries are supplied.
- The result concerns regrowth rather than autonomous boundary formation or an exergy theorem. Unconsumed feed can increase raw reactor free energy.

### P06 — Fuel-paid copying variation from a monomorphic founder

**Finite-state master-equation calculations; executed.**

Question: Can fuel-paid copying create a new realized response type and improve maintained availability?

Method: Start with six conserved material units, 12 fuel molecules and an R0 founder. Use a paired copying-error kernel and factorial reverse rates, recording the first origin of the new type.

Declared run/check counts: main and control cases: 14; possible alphabets tested: [2, 3, 4].

Finding:

- At horizon 8 and copying-error probability 0.2, final material availability is 1.3130 versus 0.9003; integrated availability is 16.2708 versus 14.3717; final occupancy probability is 0.3557 versus 0.1994.
- Paid copying supplies the first new-allele origin with probability 0.5861; the thermal-first probability is below 5e-7.
- Common-shock and no-shock controls remove or reverse the benefit. No-fuel and one-material controls coincide. Increasing the available alphabet does not monotonically increase integrated availability.

Limits:

- The finite alphabet and each type's vulnerabilities are supplied.
- Occupancy at the horizon does not mean uninterrupted survival; thermal revival remains possible.
- The model has no compartments or collective reproduction.

### P07 — Stationary recovery tails and fuel-paid first activation

**Conditional theorem and a 193-state numerical gate model; executed.**

Question: Can independent recovery opportunities reduce first-activation latency during long disturbances?

Method: Apply a Jensen survival ordering before first activation. Compare an ideal Lomax residual tail with finite Gamma-mixture gates, retaining both folded-tail and omitted-tail conventions.

Declared run/check counts: gate states: 193; Gamma bins: 192; independent gate count: 3; Jensen times independently checked: 200.

Finding:

- The stationary residual exponent is alpha-1, with alpha>1 required for normalization. At alpha=1.5, the ideal one/common response has residual exponent 0.5, while 3 independent responses have exponent 1.5.
- For the omitted-tail and renormalized verification over times 1000–10000, the apparent exponents are 0.501414 for a single response, 1.504242 for 3 independent responses and 0.501048 for 3 sites sharing a response.
- The exact first-activation stochastic ordering holds under matched finite fuel before activation.

Limits:

- Finite gates eventually have an exponential cutoff; literal infinite means belong only to the ideal limit.
- The activation model supplies retained inactive templates or basal catalysts, allowing activation from an all-inactive state. Helper-only channels cannot restart a catalyst-free state. Loss of the retained templates is not modeled, so the calculation does not establish recovery after their irreversible loss.
- The result concerns first-activation latency, not post-activation exergy or collective integrity.

### P10 — Driven X/Y reciprocal-maintenance threshold

**Deterministic reaction-network analysis with independent integration; executed.**

Question: Can reciprocal local chemistry maintain a high state against passive loss?

Method: Use a supplied well-mixed X/Y/D catalytic grammar with chemostatted fuel, finite reverse reactions and thermal sources. Independently recompute DOP853 trajectories using Radau.

Declared run/check counts: ODE cases: 9; random state identity checks: 500; symmetric fixed points: 3.

Finding:

- Symmetric fixed-point concentrations per type are 0.00137893 (stable), 0.04994922 (saddle) and 1.93052242 (stable).
- The conditioned lesions removing 25% of both types or 50% of one recover the high state. Removing either reaction pair, or setting the drive to zero, loses the high branch.
- High-state material availability is 13.80569; chemical power and dissipation are 4.63314.

Limits:

- The catalytic chemistry and reactor are supplied, with continuing reservoirs rather than finite fuel.
- This is a metabolic-feedback analogue, not a formal proof of closure of constraints, membrane formation or individuality.
- Deterministic bistability does not imply two permanent stationary laws in a finite stochastic system.

### P11 — Interface-protection baseline: pilot and fresh factorial study

**Stochastic pilot followed by prospective confirmation; executed.**

Question: Does locally produced protective material L improve template R activity through its protective effects?

Method: Use an 8×8 lattice with 8 dispersed R templates, zero L, 64 fuel packets and horizon 120. Retain 3 pilot regimes, then run 64 fresh seed rows across 4 production-by-protection arms.

Declared run/check counts: pilot histories: 36; confirmation histories: 256; unique histories: 292; events replayed: 410441.

Finding:

- Mean integrated R is 677.77 with production and protection, 202.60 with production but no protection, and 420.47 with either protection alone or neither mechanism.
- The interaction is 475.17 with standard error 18.58 and an approximate 95% interval of 438.75–511.58.
- Fuel energy per unit R-time is 13.7% lower than with production disabled, but total fuel use is higher. This ratio does not establish a total-cost efficiency advantage.

Limits:

- The initial fuel supply is finite; the study does not establish sustained through-flow.
- Produced protection is supported, while the necessity of continued production after formation is a separate question.
- No group label enters a natural rate, and contacts do not establish an enclosing membrane.
