# Criteria for Reproduction of a Physical Collective

Research audit, 21 October 2026

Fragmentation becomes candidate collective reproduction when daughters maintain and rebuild the organization, inherit variable collective traits, and repeat the process under unchanged local dynamics. An increased cluster count alone establishes none of those things.

## Operational observations

| Question | Physical observable | Required control |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Did a parent split? | An observer identifies one connected assembly becoming two or more separated assemblies. | Sweep connectivity thresholds and persistence windows; exclude one-frame bond flicker. |
| Are daughters viable organizations? | Continued catalytic activity, fuel-funded repair, material turnover, and maintained cohesion after separation. | Compare with inert fragments and randomly assembled, size-matched clusters. |
| Is there material ancestry? | Passive atom or molecule identifiers quantify material transferred from parent to each daughter. | Labels must never affect rates; distinguish ancestry from later aggregation or exchange. |
| Do daughters regenerate? | Net uptake and conversion replenish active material and permit regrowth beyond the inherited fragment. | Report actual fuel, monomers, waste, and donor depletion; no reseeding or concentration resets. |
| Is composition inherited? | Parent-daughter resemblance in variable composition or functional traits persists through regrowth and turnover. | Compare against local-pool founders matched for environment, size, age, and resource exposure. |
| Does the life cycle repeat? | Daughters regrow and produce persistent grandchildren, with documented lineage histories. | No cluster-size trigger, split command, selective relocation, or offspring allocation enters the rates. |

## Material ancestry and organizational inheritance

For parent p and daughter d, record both n_pd/N_p and n_pd/N_d, where n_pd is the amount of parent-tagged material reaching the daughter. The first measures partitioning of parental material; the second measures the daughter's material origin.

Neither fraction must remain large indefinitely: genuine growth dilutes inherited atoms. If copying transfers organization without transferring the original template's atoms, track template or copying ancestry separately. Inherited material and inherited organization are different observations.

Mergers should produce a multi-parent ancestry graph rather than being forced into an artificial family tree.

Immediate daughter similarity is expected from physically partitioning a parent. That is legitimate inheritance, but by itself it does not establish maintained collective organization. The stronger test is whether lineages retain different compositions or functional traits after regrowth and significant turnover, beyond what shared environments and random sampling from the local pool predict. Useful traits include response-type ratios, repair capacity, resource conversion, contact permeability, or regrowth and fission timing.

Do not demand heritability beyond every constituent's complete microscopic state. That conditions away the physical transmission mechanism. Nor should an identically reconstructed microscopic state behave differently merely because it lacks a preferred ancestry label.

To claim Darwinian collective individuality, additionally show heritable collective variation associated with differential collective reproduction, while reporting component-level selection and conflicts.

## Repeated reproduction within a finite budget

A practical first target is two successive generations: parent to persistent daughters to persistent grandchildren. Across replicate runs, report the probability of completing each generation; daughter survival and activity through a preregistered turnover interval; net new material and fuel cost per viable descendant; growth between division events; inherited trait distributions; and failed divisions, dissolution, mergers, and parasite takeover.

A finite-fuel model need not reproduce indefinitely. It must show the cycle within its resource budget and distinguish resource exhaustion from lack of reproductive organization.

## Adverse cases

The analysis must distinguish reproduction from ordinary coarsening, where clusters merge or larger ones grow at the expense of smaller ones; evaporation of fragments; destructive fragmentation producing progressively less active material; inherited debris persisting through long-lived bonds without active replacement; environmental copying in which similar patches recreate the same composition regardless of parent; an externally imposed life cycle; and component reproduction without persistent collective organization.

An externally imposed life cycle includes splitting at a software threshold, shaping a neck, moving daughters, choosing survivors, or replenishing them according to cluster identity.

Fragmentation caused by background shear or another environmental process is not automatically disqualified as reproduction. Natural life cycles can use environmental forcing. The forcing must be declared, physically paid, applied without consulting cluster identity or reproductive readiness, and separated from a claim of autonomous division.

## Physical precedent and next milestone

Zwicker and colleagues show that chemically driven droplet growth can generate a shape instability and division into daughters, permitting growth-division cycles without a dedicated split-the-droplet operator. This provides a direct mechanical benchmark. It does not establish inherited variable collective traits or Darwinian individuality.

[Zwicker, Seyboldt, Weber, Hyman and Juelicher, Growth and division of active droplets provides a model for protocells, Nature Physics 13, 408-413 (2017), DOI 10.1038/nphys3984](https://www.nature.com/articles/nphys3984)

For the lattice model, the corresponding milestone is that local reactions and contacts produce assemblies that physically fragment; descendants regenerate using supplied material and fuel, retain variable organizational traits through turnover, and repeat the cycle without cluster-level intervention.

Passing that test would justify reproducing collective organization. The stronger claim of an evolving collective individual then requires the heritable variation and differential-reproduction evidence. Neither test requires phenomenal consciousness or Hestia's theory-of-mind machinery.
