# When does renewal become necessary for continued life?

A protective stock can keep a system going after the process that made it has stopped. This stage asks when continued, paid production becomes necessary—and keeps a failed spatial prediction separate from a successful conditional construction.

## Two results in different models

In the finite-fuel spatial confirmation, the continuing-production effect is **4.56** template-time units, with an approximate 95% interval of **−15.55 to 24.66**. The positive interaction term does not satisfy the joint rule, which required both primary intervals above zero. The declared maintenance claim fails; inherited protection still matters over this horizon.

In a separate open, well-mixed construction, templates and protective material renew one another under supplied fuel and waste flows. A threshold, finite-reverse completion, balance checks and ablations establish maintained activity under the stated chemistry. This is a conditional mechanism, not a spatial formation result or a universal law of flourishing.

The [research checkpoint](research-checkpoint.md) retains both outcomes and exact measurements. The [theory bridge](theory-bridge.md) states the construction. The [finite-budget bound](finite-budget-bound.md) explains the closed spatial system's horizon. Catalogue records P17–P18 keep the two claims distinct.

A further [executable counterexample](path-ranking-audit.md), P19, rejects ranking arbitrary forward histories by entropy production alone. It retains the forward/reverse fluctuation relation. That refutation is more specific than rejecting thermodynamics or individuation.
