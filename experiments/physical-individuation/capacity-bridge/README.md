# What physical constraint could make specialization consequential?

The preceding [productive-phase comparison](../productive-phase/README.md) failed its formation and specialist-association claims. Complementarity alone also failed to establish a need for separate taxa: a generalist or one genotype with two phenotypes can realize the supplied functions.

This stage asks whether an actual capacity, timing or reconfiguration constraint could prevent cost-matched generalist emulation. It does not modify the failed study or add a penalty to rescue its outcome.

## A conditional bound, not a demonstrated mechanism

The [analysis](next-bridge-analysis.md), catalogue P35, proves a ceiling for a restricted synchronized module that must pass through an inactive reconfiguration state when changing productive roles. Its assumptions retain finite transition rates, work accounting and an allowed background current. Private asynchronous modules, mixed developmental states and physically available bypasses lie outside that restriction.

The certificate verifies the conditional calculation. It does not show that a real molecule or the archived lattice satisfies the required restriction. A next proposal would have to derive that incompatibility from its physical states and compare full useful renewal and costs against the best admissible alternative. No new formation experiment is reported here.

## Why the old analysis mentions Unitology

Unitology is a fictional religion in *Dead Space*. The research uses it as an adversary: collective persistence could be bought by extinguishing participants' possibilities. A chemical capacity bound cannot settle that ethical question. It also does not yet test the positive developmental sequence in which new collective and participant identities acquire wider possibilities while remaining responsible for care and costs.
