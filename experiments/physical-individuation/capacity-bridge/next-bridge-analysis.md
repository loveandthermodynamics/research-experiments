# A necessary capacity condition beyond metabolic complementarity

This is next-stage conditional theory, separate from the frozen 64-history productive-phase study. No model, protocol, prior note or physical result is changed. It identifies one possible physical obstruction to generalist emulation; it does not establish that obstruction in a molecule or our lattice.

## What the completed comparison rules out

The [joined study analysis](../productive-phase/analysis.json) fails its dense-unit claim: specialist B-mass fractions in components of at least nine sites are .203 and .259, below the declared .5 requirement. Its specialization contrast points the other way: specialist minus generalist near-dense template densities are -.118 and -.096, with intervals entirely below zero. These results constrain this supplied chemistry; they neither prove that generalists always win nor authorize a rescue scan.

The [exact comparison](../productive-phase/generalist-comparison.md) explains why complementarity alone was insufficient. Both material transformations are needed for sustained copying beyond the bounded background, but a generalist can perform both. Even half-strength generalists reproduce the ideal specialist fixed point and power; the actual spatial promiscuous control also obeys the same instantaneous forward-hazard cap. Relabeling X/Y as developmental phenotypes of one genotype preserves the complete physical path law. A proof based on complementary currents cannot infer separate taxa or a new individual.

The structural requirement is therefore **a demonstrated failure of cost-matched emulation**. It must arise from physical capabilities, timing, storage, transport or repair, rather than a penalty assigned to the word generalist. The following theorem gives one precise example. It concerns a restricted, physically synchronized module; it is NOT a ceiling on every possible generalist.

## Conditional theorem: a synchronized module pays an inactive-capacity penalty

Supply an actual physical module configuration q in {X,R,Y}. In X, only the A-to-B productive-copy family operates; in Y, only the B-to-A family operates; neither operates in the inactive reconfiguration set R. Every change from one active configuration to the other must traverse R. Direct or promiscuous bypasses are excluded only if their absence is physically justified. Private asynchronous modules and mixed developmental states are outside this premise.

Keep all productive reverse reactions, positive thermal template appearance and fixed copying variation. Let B lie between 0 and M, where M is the finite conserved A/B inventory. Configuration transitions preserve B, or their B conversions must be included in the background current. Assume the other signed A-to-B intensity obeys |j0(s)|<=L0 in every physical state. The total exit intensity from R is at most rmax>0. In each active configuration the signed net productive-copy intensities have finite upper bounds CX and CY. Configuration transitions are physical reactions with paired reverses, chemical work and energy changes obeying local detailed balance; they are not an observer controller. All bounds and environmental reservoirs must come from that same scheme.

At stationarity write K=jX+jY for net total productive copying, in events/time. The exact balances give

\[
j_X=\frac{K-j_0}{2},\qquad
j_Y=\frac{K+j_0}{2},\qquad K=l-i,
\]

where l is passive template-loss intensity and i is non-copy template-birth intensity. Assume K>L0, so both productive transformations must have positive net current. If fX,fY,fR are configuration occupation fractions, then necessarily

\[
\boxed{
\frac K2\left(\frac1{C_X}+\frac1{C_Y}\right)
-\frac{L_0}{2}\left|\frac1{C_X}-\frac1{C_Y}\right|
+\frac{K-L_0}{M r_{\max}}\le1.}
\]

For the explicitly background-free case L0=0, this is the cycle-current ceiling

\[
\boxed{J_{\rm synchronized}\le
\left(\frac1{C_X}+\frac1{C_Y}
+\frac2{M r_{\max}}\right)^{-1},\qquad J=K/2.}
\]

This sets no source or productive reverse rate to zero. The background-free case means only that no additional material-conversion channel bypasses the two productive families. The general inequality retains a positive bypass allowance.

### Proof

Partition a history into active-role blocks. A block can contain excursions into R that return to the same active role; it ends only when the other role is reached. In an X block, signed X copies equal its change in B minus its background conversions. In a Y block, signed Y copies equal minus its change in B plus background conversions. Each block can contribute at most M net copies from stored material, regardless of how long it lasts. Forward and reverse copies enter with opposite signs.

If S_T counts completed active-role changes, summing blocks yields

\[
E[C_X(T)+C_Y(T)]\le M(E[S_T]+O(1))+L_0T.
\]

The background sign is the last visited active role, determined from the past. Its stochastic compensator is bounded by L0*T because the statewise background intensity is bounded. Endpoint blocks contribute only O(M); an initially inactive module adds at most one endpoint block. Thus the asymptotic role-change frequency nu obeys nu>=(K-L0)/M.

Every completed role change requires an exit from R. The exit-count compensator is at most rmax times inactive occupation, hence fR>=nu/rmax. This uses a finite intensity bound, not a fictitious deterministic minimum waiting time: reversible stochastic transitions can still occur arbitrarily quickly on individual paths. Net active currents also give fX>=jX/CX and fY>=jY/CY. Summing these fractions to one and using |j0|<=L0 proves the display. Counts, sources and intensities are stationary expectations; this is not an almost-sure finite-population survival theorem.

## What a successful differentiated construction would have to beat

Assuming catalytic capacity scales linearly with allocated catalytic material, a relaxed parallel allocation of the same total material without reconfiguration overhead has balanced-current upper bound

\[
J_{\rm parallel}^{\rm relaxed}=
\left(1/C_X+1/C_Y\right)^{-1}.
\]

It exceeds the synchronized ceiling when M and rmax are finite. But it is only a relaxed bound: mutation, loss, transport and self-consistent material fields can prevent its attainment. A real proof must construct a maintained parallel regime above the synchronized ceiling at the same total material, volume, preparation and fuel conditions, while paying for its localization and interfaces. A one-genotype developmental mixture is an admissible parallel construction. Separate species are not required.

For example, if a matched functional requirement needs mean template number Nreq, passive loss is at least delta_min*N and non-copy appearance is at most Imax, then required net copying is at least Kreq=delta_min*Nreq-Imax. If Kreq violates the general K inequality (or Kreq/2 exceeds the J ceiling when L0=0), the restricted synchronized module cannot supply that maintenance. This is a feasibility obstruction at a fixed requirement, not a value judgment that more occupancy is intrinsically better. Large background or thermal supply can make the obstruction vacuous.

The missing physical premise is decisive. A genuinely common chemical condition might make the two catalytic tasks incompatible in one well-mixed module and require passage through an inactive material state before changing it. That is a proposed mechanism requiring an actual reaction/energy description. Merely having two enzyme conformations does NOT imply synchronization: private local switching or substrate-responsive promiscuity can evade the bound. Our current generalist has no compulsory common R state, so this theorem cannot be used to discount its observed advantage.

## Capacity, thermodynamic cost and individuality remain different

Inactive residence is a loss of productive opportunity; it is not automatically positive dissipated work per switch. A reversible barrier or equilibrium conformational excursion can have no continuing net chemical-work cost. Any work comparison must use signed fuel currents and actual energy changes. Assigning a positive fee to every gross switch would manufacture a specialization advantage and can count equilibrium back-and-forth motion incorrectly. Interface production, separation, repair, transport and preparation must be included too. The existing identity still fixes copying's contribution to chemical work from net renewal and source/loss counts; configuration work would be additional. Greater maintained abundance can require greater work.

Nor would a capacity gap itself identify an individual. Parallel tasks can be distributed through a shared bath. The additional integrity obligation is that physically generated local organization maintains the conditions of its own restoration, with matched interventions distinguishing local mutual renewal from transport and remote subsidy. Inherited regulation and informational aging remain further mechanisms. A steady chemical profile or residence-time distribution contains no demonstrated damage memory.

This is the next logical gate, not a new parameter study: identify a real emulation obstruction, verify its reversible capacity/cost scheme, and then ask whether locally differentiated renewal overcomes it. If unrestricted generalists or developmental differentiation reproduce the mechanism, retain them as successful alternatives. No universal biodiversity optimum follows.

Unitology and Resident Evil remain separate participant tests. Even a capacity-efficient, self-maintaining whole can destroy constituents' consequential interests and options. Moral legitimacy requires that these can constrain the whole and support development, including dependent interests. Voluntary fusion, dependency and sacrifice remain possible; neither permanent separation nor taxonomic richness is the criterion. No result about a synchronized chemical module derives that ethical commitment.
