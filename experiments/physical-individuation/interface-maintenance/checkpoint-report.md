# Produced Protection: Verified Results and Open Maintenance Tests

Produced protective material and paid template copying improve integrated template activity within the supplied local chemistry. The post-formation test does not establish that continued production is necessary beyond inherited protective material. Neither of two response-diversity comparisons establishes a regenerative advantage. The full biodiversity, recursive individuation and Unitology objective remains open: `full_goal_achieved` is false.

## Produced protection and copying

The baseline begins with eight dispersed templates R, zero protective material L and 64 fuel packets on an 8 by 8 lattice. Reversible local reactions produce R and L. L changes binding energy and paired passive and transport barriers. Factorial arms share initial physical resources and the Hamiltonian. Group labels, ancestry and measured morphology never change rates.

Three pilot parameter regimes were retained. A fresh 64-seed, four-arm ensemble gave these means through model time 120:

| Production of L | Protection by L | Integrated active templates | Net fuel packets consumed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Enabled | Enabled | 677.77 | 58.89 |
| Enabled | Disabled | 202.60 | 38.11 |
| Disabled | Enabled | 420.47 | 42.33 |
| Disabled | Disabled | 420.47 | 42.33 |

The paired production-by-protection interaction is 475.17 (Monte Carlo SE 18.58; approximate 95 percent interval 438.75 to 511.58) active-template-time units. Producing material without its protective barriers reduces activity here. This supports produced protection, rather than establishing necessity of continued production after formation.

Standard fuel energy used per active-template time is 13.7 percent lower than production-off; the paired bootstrap interval is 9.7 to 17.4 percent. Total fuel use is higher. This activity-normalized ratio does not establish lower total dissipation or matched-function efficiency.

The 64-seed copying ablation retains L production and protection, with identical initial states. Removing both directions of the copying channel reduces mean integrated R from 677.77 to 197.46. The paired copying benefit is 480.30 (Monte Carlo SE 20.62; approximate 95 percent interval 439.89 to 520.72). Mean first-episode persistence falls from 93.60 to 70.27; net fuel use falls from 58.89 to 26.67 packets. Three thermal R births remain across controls. Final R averages 0.125 in both arms, so the result concerns accumulated activity and persistence, not better endpoint occupancy.

Copies continue after founder loss in 58 of 64 full-arm baseline runs; R-L contacts coexist with this continuation in 56. Copying controls also survive founder turnover. Birth provenance and protective contacts do not establish a membrane, regulatory memory or collective individual.

## Continued production versus legacy protection

Every original full-arm seed is replayed to time 20, then branched into four arms for 80 further time units. All 64 preparations are retained without filtering. Each branch starts with identical material, fuel and instance tags; only observer bookkeeping is reset. Prepared R ranges from 5 to 22 and L from 8 to 20.

Protected mean integrated R is 445.54 with continued L production and 423.20 without it. The paired production effect is 22.35 (Monte Carlo SE 17.50; approximate 95 percent interval -11.95 to 56.64). The production-by-protection interaction is 36.89 (Monte Carlo SE 21.27; approximate 95 percent interval -4.80 to 78.58). Both remain inconclusive. Protection benefits continuation even after production stops: its paired effect then is 256.44 (Monte Carlo SE 15.05; approximate 95 percent interval 226.95 to 285.93). Legacy L can therefore explain much of the benefit over this horizon; a necessary ongoing maintenance loop has not been shown.

## Two response-diversity comparisons

Two energetic thermal gates provide equal marginal exposures and identical paired gate histories. R0/R1 respond to separate gates or a shared gate. Both gate energies and heat are counted. All founders are R0; copying mutation is zero or 20 percent, with rare thermal R1 births retained. Zero copying mutation is consequently not strict monoculture.

After three retained pilot hazard regimes, the original fresh 64-seed, four-arm ensemble gives a response-by-variation interaction of 20.90 (Monte Carlo SE 17.13; approximate 95 percent interval -12.67 to 54.47). Paid copying produces the first R1 in 58 of 64 differentiated/mutation runs, but a diversity advantage remains unproved.

The declared faster-copy comparison doubles copying to 0.12 in every arm, with 64 fresh seeds. Its interaction is 19.30 (Monte Carlo SE 20.33; approximate 95 percent interval -20.55 to 59.14); the absolute variation effect under differentiated hazards is 19.54 (Monte Carlo SE 22.26; approximate 95 percent interval -24.10 to 63.18). Both remain inconclusive. Row-stochastic mutation preserves aggregate forward copying mobility; reverse channels still depend on type composition. Common-hazard differences do not establish a universal diversity upkeep cost.

## Thermodynamic theorem and proof boundary

A separate conditional theorem proves that independent failure responses plus finite-fuel repair can retain more active-subsystem availability, including entropy, over a stated horizon. An independent eight-state matrix calculation verifies the finite-temperature example: the positive analytical lower bound is 0.18795 energy-time units and the computed advantage is 0.36412. The coupling proof and entropy bound are in theorem-audit.md. This does not calculate availability for the large lattice or prove universal biodiversity maximization.

More species labels need not imply more free energy. Correlations can store availability while increasing shared vulnerability. Neither dissipation nor persistence establishes participant agency or moral legitimacy: the Unitology defense still requires independently consequential participants and their interests. Ordinary turnover and finite-fuel decline do not establish aging.

## Verification and next priority

The five independent audit reports cover 1,172 unique complete trajectory records and 1,357,468 events, including 5,324 environmental gate events: baseline 292/410,441; original response 304/398,091; copying ablation 64/92,973; faster-copy response 256/242,480; maintenance 256/213,483. These totals include 84 pilot runs. Paired arms and continuations are not statistically independent replicates. The additional 64 preparation-prefix replays (19,651 events) overlap baseline history and are not counted again.

Audits reconstruct energy, propensities, reverse pairs, event ledgers, tags and gate clocks; recorded energy, heat and fuel discrepancies are zero. Randomized log detailed-balance error is below 3.4e-14. Maintenance preparation parity and all 55 paired contrasts were independently checked.

The next priority is a physically justified regime where finite-lived protective material requires paid replacement, followed by a test of ongoing reciprocal maintenance against legacy material. Existing L already decays; its residual protection currently outlasts the need demonstrated by this assay. Preserve reversible chemistry, matched resources and negative results. Establish reciprocal maintenance before attempting nested individuality. No further experiment is executed at this checkpoint; collective inheritance, regulatory memory, aging and the cosmic variational claim remain open.
