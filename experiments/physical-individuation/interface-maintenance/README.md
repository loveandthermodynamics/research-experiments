# Does continuing production sustain a living-like interface?

If activity produces a structure that protects it, does ongoing production matter after formation, or does the system merely inherit a useful stock? This stage separates those possibilities in a supplied finite-fuel chemistry. It is one prerequisite in the larger inquiry into [renewal and individuation](../README.md).

## What happened

Produced protection and fuel-paid copying improve accumulated template activity. The post-formation comparison does not establish that continuing production is necessary beyond inherited protective material. Neither of the two response-diversity comparisons establishes its proposed regenerative advantage.

Those are limits on the declared maintenance and diversity claims, not demonstrations that all regeneration or differentiation fails. Equally, the favorable activity result does not supply the missing necessity or benefit. The separate finite-fuel theorem is conditional on its own assumptions; it is not a replay of this lattice or a universal biodiversity result.

## Context for the old terminology

“Unitology” refers to a fictional religion in *Dead Space*. These notes use it to ask whether a collective could persist by consuming its participants' possibilities. Chemical persistence cannot answer that moral question. The positive hypothesis allows new members and wholes to develop identities and capacities, while the whole remains answerable for care and costs. These studies have not tested that developmental sequence. See [what the results do and do not reject](../../../docs/interpreting-results.md).
