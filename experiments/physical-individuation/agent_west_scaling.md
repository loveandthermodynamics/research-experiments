# West's scaling models and the individuation question

The defensible claim is specific: the canonical models below explain the scaling and growth of an already identified organism or urban unit. Their starting variables include its size, component units, and resource allocation. They do not derive the first formation of that unit from previously unbound constituents. This is an interpretation of these models' scope, not evidence that Geoffrey West has no account of individuation anywhere in his work.

## Three primary sources

1. **West, G. B., Brown, J. H. & Enquist, B. J. (1997). A General Model for the Origin of Allometric Scaling Laws in Biology. Science 276(5309), 122–126.** [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.276.5309.122), [author abstract indexed by PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9082983/).

   WBE derives quarter-power allometries from a space-filling, fractally branching distribution network, minimized transport-energy dissipation, and terminal tubes invariant with body size. Its principal worked system is mammalian circulation. The organism's serviced volume, network, and terminal units are therefore substantive premises. Optimization under these conditions is a mechanistic proposal for scaling; it is not a derivation of organisms from stationary action alone. The familiar prediction is metabolic power B proportional to body mass M to the 3/4 power, hence power per unit mass proportional to M^(-1/4).

2. **West, G. B., Brown, J. H. & Enquist, B. J. (2001). A general model for ontogenetic growth. Nature 413, 628–631.** [Publisher article](https://www.nature.com/articles/35098076), DOI [10.1038/35098076](https://doi.org/10.1038/35098076).

   This is not merely curve fitting: the authors allocate metabolic energy between maintaining existing tissue and creating biomass, using cellular properties to constrain growth parameters. That is directly relevant to paid maintenance and reproduction. Nevertheless, the organism, its tissue, and its cells are already specified. Ontogenetic enlargement of that organism is a different explanatory problem from why a cooperating collection becomes a new evolutionary individual.

3. **Bettencourt, L. M. A., Lobo, J., Helbing, D., Kühnert, C. & West, G. B. (2007). Growth, innovation, scaling, and the pace of life in cities. PNAS 104(17), 7301–7306.** [Publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0610172104), [full primary article at PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1852329/).

   The paper distinguishes infrastructure near exponent .8, individual needs near 1, and social outputs around 1.1–1.3. It explicitly finds no simple urban counterpart of biological quarter powers. Cities are operationally identified as integrated labor markets, using commuting boundaries. Under its growth equation, sublinear supply permits bounded growth, whereas superlinear production gives a formal singularity that real resource limits prevent. Innovation and institutional adaptation can postpone the resulting constraints; the paper does not derive symbolic institutions, their meanings, or legitimate membership from molecular metabolism. See “Scaling Relations for Urban Indicators,” “Urban Growth Equation,” and “Discussion.”

Source access: the 1997 and 2001 descriptions above were checked against the authors' abstracts, including the publisher's 2001 abstract; the 2007 full article was inspected. The first two full articles were not independently audited here.

## What should and should not be unified

An allometry Y=c M^b states how a measured quantity varies with size. Its power-law form is homogeneous under rescaling. A scale-free degree distribution is a different claim about a network, and a Pareto recovery tail is a different claim about probabilities over durations. Their exponents b, degree-tail exponent, and recovery-tail alpha cannot be substituted for one another. WBE's fixed terminal scale also matters: its idealized hierarchy is not a claim that every physical scale is interchangeable.

The biological exponent is a conditional prediction of the stated network and optimization assumptions, not an acceptance criterion requiring every chemical assembly to produce .75. The urban categories are approximate empirical summaries; .8, 1, and 1.2 should not be hard-coded as universal constants. In particular, wages and patents are social observables, not thermodynamic energy or entropy. Superlinear innovation cannot simply be relabeled greater physical negentropy.

The useful research sequence is therefore **formation first, scaling afterward**. An assembly can be a candidate higher-level individual because of its causal maintenance, reproduction, and inherited organization, even before it spans enough sizes to exhibit an allometry. Conversely, a sublinear transport cost does not establish individuality, welfare, moral standing, or preservation of constituent agents. Coercive and extractive organizations can also exploit economies of scale.

## A nested-metabolism test that retains constituent units

These are proposed diagnostics, not results from West's papers or the current prototypes:

- Identify candidate assemblies independently of the desired exponent: physically formed contacts, persistent organization, removal-and-restoration experiments, and reproducible offspring or renewal cycles. Keep component lineage identities and local repair pathways observable rather than replacing them with a single group score.
- Measure mass and material inventories, boundary exergy flows, physical transport costs, maintenance, and reproduction at both levels. Internal transfers cancel when the whole assembly's balance is summed. Count shared fuel, transport structures, bonds, and interaction energy once; a hierarchy must not create an extra reservoir through bookkeeping.
- Test whether constituent units continue their own maintenance and renewal while a larger assembly acquires a reproducible organization. Record losses, dependence, fusion, predation, and collapse as possible outcomes. Survival of a favored aggregate alone does not demonstrate preservation of its members or a further individuation transition.
- Across assemblies of different sizes, estimate external metabolic power versus total mass and network cost versus serviced units. Compare physically connected assemblies with equally provisioned disconnected aggregates. Vary spatial dimension, transport distance, feed density, and total supply separately. A well-mixed reaction model cannot by itself establish a spatial distribution-network explanation.
- Fit exponents and uncertainty over a declared finite size range; test curvature and finite-size cutoffs. Do not select cluster boundaries or discard failures to recover .75. Include actual active availability and renewal alongside power: low power can mean efficiency, dormancy, starvation, or failure.

For symbolic institutions, add a distinct causal layer: maintained records, roles, rules, communications, and mechanisms that reproduce or revise membership and coordination. Price their physical implementation and test how their intervention changes constituent and collective renewal. A superlinear social statistic can motivate that mechanism; it cannot supply the mechanism or its ethical justification.

The connection to the present agenda is consequently conditional: a new collective may form through paid interactions, then develop a distribution architecture with measurable scaling economies while retaining lower-level maintenance. West provides mechanisms and quantitative diagnostics for the latter step. The first step and its recursive repetition require additional theory and evidence.
