# Identifying a continuing organization without assuming an individual

## Relevant prior work

[Krakauer, Bertschinger, Olbrich, Flack and Ay (2020)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12064-020-00313-7)
provide a quantitative information-theoretic account of individuality. It is
continuous, permits nested and distributed units, and does not require cell
walls. Their temporal measures include predictive information
A*=I(S_next;S_now), conditional autonomy A=I(S_next;S_now|E_now), and environmental
determination I(S_next;E_now|S_now). These distinguish different system–environment
dependencies rather than declaring one universal anatomical boundary.

The authors explicitly distinguish identifying individuality from generating
it. Their discussion leaves the time-series generator, robust self-preservation
and an optimal boundary-induction algorithm as further requirements. This is
therefore useful prior art for the observer's measurement problem, not the
missing physical derivation of self-maintenance. It directly fits the user's
holarchic concern while leaving the local-maintenance experiment necessary.

## A mathematical limit of the readouts

Let S(t)=Z be one random bit that never changes; take its environment independent
of Z. Then I(S_future;S_past)=1 bit, conditional autonomy is also 1 bit and
environmental determination is zero. These are perfect temporal-persistence
scores. They do not demonstrate metabolic renewal, adaptation, a maintained
regulator or a new living individual. The example is our diagnostic, not a
claim that the authors overlooked their stated generator requirement.

Likewise, ordinary geometric contact and descendant tags are insufficient.
A binding interaction can cause a patch, and copying can preserve a lineage,
without preserving a differentiated regulatory organization.

## Operational use in this research

The physical generator must come first: paid production, resource access,
turnover, transport and a causal return to continued activity. Information
measures can then identify which candidate coarse descriptions retain useful
temporal structure across that turnover. Interventions should test whether
the proposed organization restores capacities and whether purported boundaries
track causal dependence, rather than merely label dense regions.

Both spatial and temporal resolution need sensitivity analysis. A co-moving
organization need not remain inside one fixed observation box. A nested unit
need not be maximally isolated or free of dependence on its environment.
Constituent and collective descriptions can both carry predictive information.

No such mutual-information estimate is claimed for the current lattice data.
High-dimensional state alphabets and few samples would make naive plugin
estimates unreliable. A later identification test needs declared coarse
variables and time lags, held-out prediction, controls for shared environmental
drive and sampling bias, and physical perturbations. An information score must
not feed back into the reaction rates as a reward for looking individual.

The present readouts—activity, turnover, contact excess over a fixed-count
mixing null, and conservative transport interventions—supply parts of this
assessment. They do not replace the missing identification and robustness
tests or establish participant agency.
