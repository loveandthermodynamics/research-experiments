# From sustained chemistry toward differentiated individuals

The proposed causal chain is: supplied free energy sustains reciprocal renewal; molecular interactions localize that renewal into finite units; paid copying generates productive differences; those differences help renew the units that support them; inherited regulation preserves function through damage and replacement. Repeating that connection could produce new levels of individuality. Our evidence now establishes parts of this chain in separate models. It has not joined the chain, derived recursive individuation or established a universal biodiversity optimum.

The first physical step has substance. The [reciprocal-maintenance construction](../renewal-turnover/theory-bridge.md) has an internally generated high-state threshold, reversible reactions, explicit continuing fuel work and intervention controls. Maintained populations can outlast original chemical instances. This is conditional on supplied interactions and reaction capabilities. The spatial studies add paid copying and locally produced protection, without an observer-defined group changing natural rates. They show how maintenance can operate through material turnover, rather than treating organizational integrity as an inventory of permanent parts.

The next connection remains adverse evidence. In the [fresh differentiated-renewal study](../differentiated-renewal/checkpoint.md), paid mutant descendants appeared and sometimes established, but the declared response-diversity benefit and protection interaction failed their joint criterion. Different vulnerabilities did not demonstrably fund stronger organization. A separate intervention did support nearby productive activity: sham minus near blockade increased local template-time by 23.01, with a 95% interval from 6.27 to 39.76. That is fixed-location support under initially hazard-matched, strength-varying reversible blockade. It establishes neither a self-bounded owner nor a diversity advantage. Global descendant survival, local transport and founder-instance turnover remain different observations.

The [active-phase study](study-design.md) targets a physical unit rather than assuming one. Its 48 fixed trajectories start with soluble carriers, no B phase and mobile binding enzymes. The [completed analysis](analysis.json) fails the joint criterion: full late B densities are 3.68% and 3.26% in the two domains, with only .79% and 3.93% of B mass in clusters of at least nine sites. Some wavelength gates pass, but the full regime mainly sustains dilute chemical cycling, not demonstrated dense finite units. An undefined required early-window quantity remains nonestimable rather than being dropped. This constrains the supplied regime, not every driven-phase mechanism: turnover must be balanced against material accumulation, not maximized. Independent replay verifies every physical history and proposal, plus the measurements and statistics. Its t=200 B-carrier cohort records chemical-state cycling: a carrier can convert to A and return to B. Final B membership is not uninterrupted persistence, copied ancestry or regulatory memory.

The [productive-role theorem](productive-roles-bridge.md) supplies a more specific next bridge. One copying activity consumes A and supplies B; another consumes B and returns A. Both copies use precursor and fuel, and fixed copying error can generate the alternative from an ancestor. Their homogeneous ideal reduction has stable coexistence with finite reverse reactions and positive thermal appearance. A separate finite-state balance law bounds the stationary mean template count after either productive-family blockade by `(maximum absolute background net A/B current + maximum total non-copy template birth intensity) / minimum per-template loss`. This includes every thermal or external source; an instantaneous count can exceed its expectation bound. Complementary transformations can therefore be necessary for renewal without rewarding diversity. They need not belong to separate taxa: a generalist, or one genotype with developmental specialization, could supply both. Exploiters and bypass pathways remain live alternatives. Spatial partitioning must still connect this chemistry to actual unit formation and turnover.

Informational aging requires a further mechanism. Chemical instance loss, carrier cycling and finite-fuel exhaustion are insufficient. A regulatory state must carry consequential information about damage or functional history, with physical costs for retaining, correcting and transmitting it. Lifetime comparisons must hold useful function or survival requirements fixed and count setup, repair, transport and replacement. Otherwise death can trivially reduce dissipation. The earlier renewal-cost reasoning allows a finite optimal replacement age under stated costs; it does not establish a universal preference for maximum longevity.

The [stochastic-action bridge](../driven-spatial/stochastic-action-bridge.md) describes typical dynamics conditional on a supplied generator. Its zero-action trajectories minimize fluctuation cost, not dissipated heat or Hamilton's mechanical action. Continuing positive dissipation is compatible with zero chemical fluctuation action. This is a useful variational description of a mechanism; it cannot choose the chemistry or make every persistent whole good.

Unitology and Resident Evil therefore remain internal tests. Assimilation, replication and efficient collective survival can satisfy physical organization criteria while destroying participants' consequential independence. The ethical requirement is that their capacities and interests can still constrain and redirect the whole, supporting development rather than compulsory incorporation. That permits voluntary fusion, dependency and sacrifice without treating every boundary as sacred. The full account must connect differentiated renewal to that participant criterion. Neither complexity, taxonomic richness nor thermodynamic efficiency alone supplies it.
