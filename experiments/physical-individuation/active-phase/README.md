# Can driven material cycling form a maintained unit?

Renewal has to produce an organization before we can ask what new capacities that organization enables. This stage supplies conserved enzymes and carriers, fuel-driven chemistry and attractive material interactions. It asks whether that model forms dense, active units; the enzymes do not copy in this construction.

B denotes the model’s sticky working material.

## What the formation test found

The **48** fixed trajectories show driven material cycling, but the dense-unit criterion fails. Mean late B site densities are **3.68%** and **3.26%** at the two tested sizes. An undefined early full-arm side-16 spectral quantity leaves two required growth gates nonestimable; they cannot be replaced with favorable values. Renewal counts or a static aggregate do not satisfy the missing joint criterion.

This is a failed formation claim for the supplied model and declared readouts. A separate homogeneous productive-role calculation and source/loss bound establish conditional algebra, not successful spatial formation or a necessity for separate specialist taxa.

## The wider developmental proposal

The old Unitology (*Dead Space*) and *Resident Evil* references ask whether a whole could succeed by extinguishing its members' possibilities. They are fictional adversaries, not the hypotheses executed here. The positive proposal is new organization and capacities that can develop participants as well as the whole, with care and accountability through changes of identity. This failed chemical formation test neither demonstrates nor completes a test of that larger proposal. [Interpretation and discriminating future tests](../../../docs/interpreting-results.md).
